Fine Pitch Patterning and mSAP Process Innovations in High-Density Substrates

Overcoming Etch Undercut: Subtractive vs mSAP

Conventional subtractive processing etches thick copper foils, but fails below 20um Line/Space (L/S) due to severe Undercut defects. To fabricate fine-pitch interconnects on FC-BGA substrates, manufacturers rely on the Modified Semi-Additive Process (mSAP).

Step-by-Step mSAP Process Flow

  1. Seed Layer Deposition: Thin electroless copper (~1um) is deposited over the ABF dielectric substrate.
  2. Dry Film Photolithography: Dry Film Resist (DFR) is laminated, exposed, and developed to define circuit channels.
  3. Copper Electroplating: High-purity copper is electroplated inside the photoresist trenches with micro-meter precision.
  4. DFR Stripping & Seed Etch: DFR is stripped away, followed by a rapid flash-etch to dissolve the thin seed layer without damaging circuit traces.

Scaling Challenges Below 5/5um Line/Space

Pushing circuit scaling below 5/5um risks trace delamination due to reduced interfacial adhesion. Engineering nanometer-scale surface roughness on ABF dielectrics while managing ultra-thin seed layer etching is critical to maintaining pattern integrity.
